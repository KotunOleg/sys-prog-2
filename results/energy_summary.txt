Energy measurement methodology:
- Host: Apple Silicon Mac (native arm64 build, bin_macos/), not the VM (VirtualBox has no RAPL/energy counters)
- Tool: sudo powermetrics -i 100 --samplers cpu_power,tasks --show-process-energy
- Workload: identical for all 4 variants: N=700, REPEAT=20 (same total amount of work)
- Power averaged ONLY over samples where the matmul process was present in the process
  table (active samples), to avoid dilution by idle padding (2s sleep before/after the run)
- Energy (J) = avg_active_power(W) * wall_clock_time(s)

Results (N=700, REPEAT=20, identical workload across all variants):

variant     wall_time_s   avg_active_power_W   n_active_samples   energy_J   speedup_vs_O0naive   energy_reduction_vs_O0naive
O0_naive    17.72         7.205                185                127.673   1.00x                 1.00x
O0_ikj       8.95         7.296                 80                 65.300   1.98x                 1.96x
O3_naive     5.66         4.490                 51                 25.415   3.13x                 5.02x
O3_ikj       0.48         7.016                  4                  3.368  36.92x                37.91x

Key finding: energy savings (~38x) closely tracks the time speedup (~37x), not a
reduction in instantaneous power draw (avg_active_power_W stays in a narrow
4.5-7.3 W band across all variants -- the CPU core runs near its performance
state regardless of algorithm). This means SIMD/cache optimizations here save
energy primarily by finishing the same work faster, not by drawing less power
while running.

Caveat: O3_ikj run (0.48s) only yielded 4 active power samples at 100ms
sampling interval -- high measurement uncertainty for that one data point.
For a production-grade measurement, REPEAT should be scaled up so even the
fastest variant runs for several seconds, trading off "identical workload
size" for "identical measurement duration". Both approaches are documented
here.
